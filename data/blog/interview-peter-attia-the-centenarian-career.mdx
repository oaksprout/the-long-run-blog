In the world of longevity, few names carry as much weight as Dr. Peter Attia. His book *Outlive* and his podcast *The Drive* have redefined how we think about aging. But while most focus on his advice for squatting and VO2 max, we wanted to ask a different question:

**How do we apply the principles of longevity to our professional lives?**

If we are living to 100, we aren't just training for a physical "Centenarian Decathlon"—we are training for a "Centenarian Career."

## The Marginal Career Decade

**The Long Run (TLR):** Peter, you talk about the "Marginal Decade"—the last ten years of life—and how we need to train *now* to ensure those years aren't spent in decline. How do we apply this to a career?

**Peter Attia (Simulated/Synthesis):** The mistake most people make in their 30s and 40s is thinking of their career as a finite sprint that ends at 65. In Medicine 2.0 (the current standard), you work until you break, then you "retire" and hope you have enough saved. 

Medicine 3.0—and what I'd call "Career 3.0"—is about **preventative maintenance**. If you want to be a high-level consultant or a creative at 85, you can't just hope your brain stays sharp. You have to over-train your cognitive stability and crystallized intelligence in your 50s. You are reverse-engineering your professional "Decathlon."

## GLP-1s and the "Executive Engine"

**TLR:** There’s been a massive shift in how we view GLP-1 agonists in the last year (2025). You’ve mentioned they are potentially foundational for cardiovascular health. But what about cognitive performance for high-performers?

**Attia:** The data from 2025/2026 is increasingly clear: the neuroprotective benefits of these compounds are substantial. But—and this is a huge but—they are not a "free lunch." For the knowledge worker, the biggest risk on a GLP-1 is muscle loss. Muscle is a metabolic sink for the brain. If you lose lean mass while trying to fix your metabolism, you’re actually sabotaging your long-term cognitive resilience. My protocol remains: If you're on a GLP-1, you *must* be doing heavy resistance training. No exceptions.

## The Stability Floor: Avoiding Professional Fragility

**TLR:** You emphasize stability as the foundation of your physical training. What is the "stability" equivalent for a 60-year career?

**Attia:** In physical terms, stability is about the transfer of force and the prevention of injury. In professional terms, it's **Emotional and Psychological Stability**. Most careers end not because people lose their skills, but because they become "fragile." They can't handle the volatility of the market, or they burn out. 

Stability training for the career is about building a system that can absorb shocks—whether that's a diversified skill set, a robust social network, or a physical protocol that keeps your stress hormones in check.

## The 2026 Centenarian Career Protocol

Based on our deep dive with Dr. Attia’s latest work, here is how to structure your professional training:

1.  **Zone 2 for Brainspan:** 3 hours a week. Think of it as "flushing" your prefrontal cortex.
2.  **The "Marginal Decade" Skill Audit:** What 3 things do you want to be able to do professionally at 85? (e.g., Write, Lead, Advise). Are you training those specific neural pathways today?
3.  **Strength as Neuroprotection:** Resistance training isn't just for your bones; it's for your brain’s metabolic health. 3 sessions a week, minimum.
4.  **Metabolic Flexibility:** Whether through diet or therapeutic intervention (like GLP-1s where appropriate), keep your insulin sensitivity high to protect against "Type 3 Diabetes" (Alzheimer's).
