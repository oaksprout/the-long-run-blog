# Research Report: Financial and Social Longevity (2024-2026)

## Overview
This report synthesizes findings from five key sources published between 2024 and 2026, focusing on the intersection of financial planning for a 100-year life and the social determinants of health (SDOH). As lifespans extend, the "Medicine 3.0" approach to wealth requires a proactive, long-term strategy that accounts for health equity and rising medical costs.

## Key Themes for 2024-2026

### 1. Longevity Literacy and the "New Map of Life"
The Stanford Center on Longevity and Corebridge Financial (2024) identify a significant "longevity gap" where Americans desire long lives but lack the financial planning to fund them beyond age 67.
*   **Key Insight:** A 100-year life necessitates a multi-stage existence with flexible work roles and lifelong learning to maintain "cognitive capital."
*   **Application:** Financial education must start early and focus on funding 30-40 years of retirement through strategic career pivots.

### 2. The Decumulation Challenge
The World Economic Forum (2025) emphasizes a shift from wealth *accumulation* to *decumulation*.
*   **Key Insight:** Managing spending over a 30+ year retirement is more complex than saving, particularly with the risks of cognitive decline.
*   **Protocol:** Transitioning retirement systems to handle lifelong income streams and enhancing "longevity literacy" to prevent late-life financial depletion.

### 3. Health as a Financial Asset (SDOH)
Both the WHO (2025) and GSA (2024) position Social Determinants of Health as the primary drivers of financial longevity.
*   **Key Insight:** Inequities in income, environment, and social connection are financial risks. Poor health due to SDOH leads to early career exit and high medical costs.
*   **Longevity Fitness:** Achieving "fitness" in a 100-year life requires simultaneous strength in health, social networks, and financial resources.

### 4. The Health-Wealth Inflation Gap
J.P. Morgan Private Bank (2025) highlights that healthcare and long-term care costs are rising significantly faster than general inflation.
*   **Strategic Moves:** Recommended use of Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) and strategic Social Security claiming (waiting until age 70) to create a robust income floor for the centenarian years.

## Synthesis of Findings (2026 Context)
1.  **Medicine 3.0 for Wealth:** Just as Medicine 3.0 focuses on prevention, "Wealth 3.0" focuses on protecting the "Executive Engine" through cognitive health and social stability to extend the earning years.
2.  **Equity is Essential:** Financial planning can no longer be viewed in isolation from social determinants. Access to clean environments and strong social networks reduces the "biological cost" of aging, which in turn preserves financial assets.
