# Research Report: Theobromine and P7C3-A20 (Late 2025/Early 2026)

## 1. Theobromine and Biological Aging
- **Study Title:** [Implied: Theobromine levels and epigenetic aging markers]
- **Journal:** *Aging*
- **Publication Date:** December 10, 2025
- **Lead Researcher:** Professor Jordana Bell, King's College London
- **Key Findings:**
    - **Metabolic Link:** Higher circulating levels of theobromine (a primary alkaloid in cocoa) are significantly associated with a lower biological age compared to chronological age.
    - **Mechanism:** The study used DNA methylation patterns and telomere length as markers. Theobromine showed specific anti-aging correlations that other cocoa/coffee metabolites did not.
    - **Significance:** Suggests specific nutritional strategies involving theobromine for extending healthspan and slowing biological decay.
- **Authority:** High (King's College London, TwinsUK, and KORA cohorts).

## 2. P7C3-A20 and Alzheimer's Reversal
- **Study Title:** [Implied: P7C3-A20 restores NAD+ homeostasis to reverse Alzheimer's disease]
- **Journal:** *Cell Reports Medicine*
- **Publication Date:** December 22, 2025 (Widely reported Jan 2026)
- **Lead Researcher:** Andrew Pieper, Case Western Reserve University
- **Key Findings:**
    - **NAD+ Homeostasis:** P7C3-A20 restores the brain's NAD+ balance without elevating it to supraphysiologic levels, which is a safer approach than some direct NAD+ precursors.
    - **Disease Reversal:** In mouse models of advanced Alzheimer's, the compound reversed cognitive decline, restored blood-brain barrier (BBB) integrity, and reduced tau phosphorylation and neuroinflammation.
    - **Neurogenesis:** Promotes the survival of newly generated hippocampal neurons, essential for memory restoration.
    - **Clinical Outlook:** Paving the way for human clinical trials as a potential "Medicine 3.0" intervention for neurodegeneration.
- **Authority:** High (Case Western Reserve University, *Cell Reports Medicine*).
