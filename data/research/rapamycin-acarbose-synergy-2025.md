# Research Report: Rapamycin + Acarbose Synergy (ITP 2025 Review)

## Executive Summary
The 2025 review of the National Institute on Aging's Interventions Testing Program (ITP), published in the *Journal of Gerontology: Biological Science*, marks 20 years of standardized aging research. A primary highlight of this review is the robust synergistic effect of combining **Rapamycin** (an mTOR inhibitor) with **Acarbose** (an alpha-glucosidase inhibitor). This combination has produced some of the most significant lifespan extensions in the program's history.

## Key Findings: Lifespan Extension
The synergy between Rapamycin and Acarbose results in lifespan extensions that significantly exceed the effects of either drug administered individually.

*   **Median Lifespan Increase (starting at 9 months):**
    *   **Females:** ~28% increase.
    *   **Males:** ~34% increase (with some cohorts reaching up to 37%).
*   **Late-Life Intervention (starting at 16 months):**
    *   The combination still yields a significant **13% increase** in median lifespan for both sexes, demonstrating efficacy even when initiated in middle-to-late age.

## Biological Mechanisms of Synergy
The synergy is attributed to the simultaneous modulation of two critical nutrient-sensing and metabolic pathways.

1.  **mTOR Inhibition (Rapamycin):**
    *   Rapamycin inhibits the **mTORC1** (mechanistic Target of Rapamycin Complex 1) pathway.
    *   This inhibition mimics the effects of caloric restriction, induces **autophagy** (cellular cleanup), and reduces systemic inflammation (SASP).
2.  **Insulin Sensitivity & Glucose Blunting (Acarbose):**
    *   Acarbose inhibits alpha-glucosidase in the small intestine, slowing the breakdown of complex carbohydrates.
    *   This **blunts postprandial glucose spikes** and lowers the resulting insulin response, improving overall **insulin sensitivity**.
3.  **Synergistic Interaction:**
    *   The combination targets both the nutrient-sensing (mTOR) and the metabolic (glucose/insulin) hallmarks of aging.
    *   **Acarbose acts as a "buffer"** for Rapamycin: Long-term or high-dose Rapamycin can sometimes cause glucose intolerance or insulin resistance as a side effect (via mTORC2 inhibition). Acarbose mitigates this by improving glucose handling, allowing for the benefits of mTOR inhibition without the metabolic downside.
