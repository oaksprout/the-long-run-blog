## Executive Summary
As of early 2026, taurine has moved from a "deficiency-driven aging" hypothesis (2023) to a more nuanced "metabolic-longevity regulator" framework. While a landmark NIH study in 2025 challenged its role as a universal biomarker for aging, clinical evidence continues to mount for its specific benefits in muscle preservation and metabolic health.

## 1. Scientific Sources (2025-2026 Synthesis)
The following sources form the basis of this report, focusing on research published or analyzed in the 2025-2026 period:

1.  **National Institutes of Health (NIH) - June 2025:** A large-scale cross-species study (mice, monkeys, humans) determined that circulating taurine levels are not consistent biomarkers of aging, as levels were found to be stable or even increased in some aging cohorts, challenging the 2023 "taurine deficiency" model of aging.
2.  **Human Skeletal Muscle Metabolism Study - August 2025:** A randomized trial showed that 7 days of high-dose taurine (5g/day) did not immediately increase muscle taurine content or fat oxidation but significantly improved post-exercise amino acid profile and recovery markers.
3.  **Metabolic Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis (2025):** Analysis of long-term supplementation data revealed significant reductions in triglycerides, total cholesterol, and HbA1c in overweight and obese adults, confirming taurine's role as a potent metabolic regulator.
4.  **Japanese Longitudinal Study (2024-2025 Analysis):** Confirmed a strong correlation between dietary taurine intake and maintenance of knee extension strength in middle-aged and older adults over an 8-year follow-up period.

## 2. Muscle Preservation
Taurine's role in skeletal muscle is critical due to its concentration in muscle tissue and its function in calcium ion handling.

*   **Regenerative Capacity:** 2025 research indicates that taurine can counteract the age-related decline in skeletal muscle regeneration by supporting satellite cell function.
*   **Mechanical Function:** By increasing calcium ion uptake into muscle cells and optimizing the delay in muscle relaxation, taurine supports peak force production.
*   **The "Loading" Requirement:** The August 2025 human trial suggests that short-term (1-week) loading is insufficient to change muscle concentrations, indicating that **long-term adherence (3+ months)** is required for structural muscle preservation benefits.

## 3. Metabolic Health
Taurine acts as a "longevity amino acid" through several metabolic pathways:

*   **Lipid Management:** Supplements significantly lower triglycerides and total cholesterol.
*   **Glycemic Control:** 3g/day has been shown to lower HbA1c and improve HOMA-IR (insulin resistance) in clinical populations.
*   **Enzymatic Discovery (2024-2025):** The identification of the PTER enzyme, which converts N-acetyltaurine back to taurine, has linked taurine metabolism directly to BMI regulation and genetic metabolic efficiency.

## 4. 2026 Dosage Protocols
Based on the synthesis of 2025-2026 clinical data, the following protocols are recommended for healthy adults:

### A. The "Longevity Foundation" Protocol
*   **Goal:** General healthspan extension and anti-inflammatory support.
*   **Dosage:** 1.5g total per day.
*   **Timing:** 500mg taken three times daily (morning, afternoon, evening) to maintain steady plasma levels.

### B. The "Metabolic Optimization" Protocol
*   **Goal:** Improving lipid profile, insulin sensitivity, and biological age.
*   **Dosage:** 3g to 6g per day.
*   **Note:** Doses above 3g are typically reserved for those with elevated triglycerides or HbA1c.

### C. The "Athletic/Muscle Recovery" Protocol
*   **Goal:** Muscle damage prevention and recovery.
*   **Dosage:** 1g taken 5 times daily (for high-volume endurance) OR 3-5g taken 60-120 minutes before exercise.

### Safety Note
In 2026, the consensus for upper-level intake (UL) remains at **6g/day** for long-term safety, with no significant adverse effects reported at these levels in clinical trials.
